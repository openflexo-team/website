# Formose

Problem: Critical complex systems need a formally-grounded requirements engineering (RE) method
that accounts for their architectural complexity, integrates verification and validation
techniques to ensure the quality of requirements, and gives systematic guidelines and tool
support for elaborating high-quality requirements models.

Contribution: The Formose ANR project (ANR-14-CE28-0009, 2014-2019) designed a formal, model-based
requirements engineering method for critical complex systems, supported by an open-source
environment. It defined a multi-view requirements modeling language extending existing RE
languages with the abstract architecture of complex systems and with requirements for safety,
performance and reconfiguration modes, together with translation rules to formal verification
tools such as B and UPPAAL. The supporting platform was built on
[OpenFlexo](https://openflexo.org), integrating the tools developed during the project with the
existing provers. The consortium brought together two academic partners (Institut Mines-Télécom
and LACL) and three companies (ClearSy, OpenFlexo, and THALES) around two industrial case
studies. The project ended in 2019; its tools, documentation and examples remain available for
download.
